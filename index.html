Savings account
Birbank

The flow

Seven screens carry the customer from discovery to a funded, personalized goal earning interest. Each step removes one reason to abandon.

01

Products listing

The products are introduced as a bottom sheet from the home feed itself — the business side highlights the new product

02

Product info

Rate, compounding and withdrawal terms are stated plainly, with a digital deposit shown as the one relevant alternative — no product‑catalogue sprawl.

03

Branch‑free KYC

A short self‑recorded video replaces in‑person verification. The copy is deliberately reassuring about privacy — this is usually where trust breaks down.

04

Sign, don't visit

Every document required to open the account is reviewed and e‑signed in‑app, replacing what used to be a paper stack at a branch counter.

05

Product cabinet

The product is introduced as a bottom sheet from the home feed itself — the rate and the benefit lead, before any commitment is asked for.

06

Goal customization

Before the account even exists, the customer names what it's for. A category and a target amount turn an abstract percentage into "Traveling to Japan."

07

The goal, funded

After the first top‑up, the home screen becomes the goal itself — progress bar, interest earned, and payment history all framed around "Traveling to Japan."

Loan order
Birbank

The flow

Nine screens carry the customer from a home‑feed loan tile to approved, disbursed cash — with a debt‑consolidation option surfaced before a plain new loan.

01 — Discovery

A tile, not a menu

The loan product sits inside the "Order bank products" tab the customer already uses for cards, savings and insurance — not buried in a separate section.

02 — Education

Rate, limit, term

The same three numbers that matter for any loan — from 10.9%, up to 50,000 ₼, up to 59 months — stated before anything is asked of the customer.

03 — Option

Consolidate or borrow fresh

Customers with debt at other banks are offered a consolidation path first, with their existing balance shown inline — a plain new loan is still one tap away.

04 — Configure

Every cost, before consent

Amount, term, interest, monthly payment and commission are all visible on one screen, with the strikethrough showing exactly what the fee changes.

05 — Transparency

Where the money actually goes

For consolidation loans, a numbered breakdown shows what pays off other banks now versus what arrives after debts are closed — removing the "wait, why isn't it all mine?" moment.

06 — Identity

The same trusted step

Loan applications reuse the same branch‑free biometric verification as the savings flow — one consistent moment of trust across every credit product.

07 — Consent

Reviewed and signed

The loan agreement is reviewed and e‑signed in‑app, the same consent pattern the customer already learned from other products.

08 — Disbursement

Choose the payout, confirm once

The customer sees the exact amount they'll receive now and which card it lands on, then confirms with a single "Təsdiq et."

09 — Approved

Clear next step, not just a checkmark

Approval is confirmed instantly, with a plain reminder of the one remaining action — closing other banks' debts within 10 days — instead of leaving the customer to wonder what happens next.

Cash loan personal cabinet
Birbank

The flow

Three screens carry an existing borrower from "what do I owe today" to a fully transparent repayment schedule and back to a reassuring, paid‑ahead state.

01 — Due today

The one number that matters

On a payment day, the cabinet leads with the exact amount due and a countdown, with Pay one tap away — no digging through statements.

Sidebar and Header redesign
Yelo Bank

The problem

Internet Banking's sidebar was built to list features as they shipped, one flat item after another. That worked fine early on — but every new feature meant one more line in an already long list. By the time the feature set matured, the sidebar was a wall of icons with no hierarchy: a customer signature product like HÖP (government payments) sat visually equal to a settings link, and finding anything meant scanning the whole column.

Before → After

The fix wasn't to add more items — it was to group the ones already there. Related actions now live under a shared category the customer can expand or collapse, so the default view stays short even as the underlying feature count keeps growing.

Before

One long column

Every feature — transfers, payroll, guarantees, templates, government payments — sat at the same level, in the order it happened to ship.

After

Grouped by task

Transfers, payments, payroll and guarantees each collapse into their own category, cutting the default list roughly in half without hiding anything a click away.

What else changed

The header picked up the pieces the old one was missing at this scale: a live currency indicator, a notification bell, and the account holder's name and signature right paired together instead of split across the layout — small additions that matter more once a business account has more than one signer.

Mobile POS order
Yelo Bank

The flow

Eight screens carry a business customer from a promo offer to a signed, confirmed Mobile POS order — with the State Tax Service check and receipt details handled before any signature is asked for.

01 — Discovery

One offer, one action

Mobile POS surfaces as a bottom sheet from the home feed, leading with the perks — free onboarding, no turnover requirement, a complimentary business card — before asking anything of the customer.

02 — Consent

One data check, made explicit

Before any paperwork starts, the customer explicitly consents to a State Tax Service (DVX) lookup on their registered business, with a plain-language explanation of what an “economic subject (object)” means one tap away.

03 — Object

Confirm what's on file

Account code and registered address are pulled in automatically and shown for confirmation, removing manual entry for details the bank already holds.

04 — Receipt details

What the receipt will say

Business name, address and the settlement account are set here — the exact details that will print on every customer receipt — with the account defaulting to the customer's current account.

05 — Contact

Where the setup instructions land

A mobile number and email are confirmed for delivery — the same number that will receive the activation SMS — with a reminder not to use a number registered to another operator.

06 — Review

Everything in one place

Every detail collected so far — account code, business name and address, settlement account, phone and email — is shown together for a final check before signing.

07 — Sign

Sign, don't visit

Agreement, application and appendix are reviewed and e-signed in one tap, the same in-app consent pattern used across other Yelo Bank products.

08 — Confirmation

Live in three business days

The order is confirmed instantly, with a clear expectation set: setup instructions and login details arrive by SMS within three business days.

Guarantee order
Yelo Bank

The flow

Seven screens carry a business customer through applying for a tender guarantee — from a promo card in the sidebar to a signed, confirmed application, with terms and collateral handled on the bank's side rather than the customer's.

01 — Discovery

Surfaced from the sidebar

The guarantee product appears as a promo card the moment a business customer opens the new Qarantiyalar section — rate, limit and commission stated up front, before any form starts.

02 — Link

One link, not a form

The application starts with just the tender's public link from the e‑tender portal — not a re‑typed copy of details that are already published elsewhere.

03 — Auto-fill

Details pulled, not typed

Once the link resolves, the tender's name, beneficiary, VÖEN and address populate on their own — the customer reviews rather than transcribes.

04 — Order details

Terms and collateral, one screen

Guarantee dates, amount, the automatically calculated commission, and which account to draw from are all set here before anything is signed.

05 — Documents

Upload only what's missing

Just the two documents the bank doesn't already hold — the terms package and the founding decision — need uploading, with clear format and size limits.

06 — Sign

Three documents, reviewed in place

The consent application, guarantee agreement and collateral agreement are each opened and signed without leaving the flow.

07 — Confirmation

A same-day promise

The application is confirmed instantly, with a plain expectation set — it will appear on the portal the same day — and a direct link to track its status.

Pre-drive inspection fee
Yelo Bank

The flow

Three screens carry a business customer with a vehicle fleet from a list of contracts to a single confirmed payment — covering every vehicle's inspection fee at once instead of one at a time.

01 — Contracts

Every contract, one view

Business customers see every active vehicle‑fleet contract tied to their VÖEN, each with its own vehicle count and pre‑calculated total, before choosing which to pay.

02 — Vehicles

Every vehicle, itemized

Tapping the vehicle count opens a full breakdown — each plate number with its own fee and commission — so nothing is paid as an opaque lump sum.

03 — Payment

Two accounts, one confirmation

The customer picks which account pays the fee and which covers the commission, with both amounts shown plainly before confirming.

Kiron
Therapist‑matching platform

Overview

Kiron helps people find a therapist who actually fits them — by specialty, language, availability and approach — instead of scrolling an unfiltered directory. This was a full web design pass: personas, type and color system, a responsive grid, and complete desktop and mobile builds for the patient‑facing site.

Personas first

Two grounded personas — Aylin, a marketing specialist quietly overwhelmed by burnout, and Kamran, an HR manager whose long hours leave no room to search — shaped every decision that followed: what the platform needed to prove before someone would trust it with something this personal.

System & screens

DM Sans carries the whole interface — geometric, clean, legible at a glance — paired with a calm palette (soft sky, iced blue, calm gray) built to feel steady rather than clinical. The grid, desktop layout, and mobile flow that follow all use it consistently, from the "How Kiron works" steps to the therapist selection and blog sections.
